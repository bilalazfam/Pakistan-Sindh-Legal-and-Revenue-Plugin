# Pleading checklists (Sindh civil courts)

Every rule quoted below was checked against the official CPC text in `statutes/CPC_1908.txt`
(Pakistan Code, downloaded October 2026). Points that rest on case law rather than the text are marked
**[case law: confirm authority]**: never fill those with a citation from memory.

## 1. Supreme Court guidelines on civil pleadings (May 2026)

Review petition of *Fazeelat Nasreen & others*, Supreme Court of Pakistan (Shahid Bilal Hassan and
Shakeel Ahmad, JJ.), reported in the press on 19-05-2026. **Get the judgment and its citation before
quoting it** [case law: confirm citation]. As reported, the trial court is to check on presentation:

**Plaint**
- [ ] All particulars required by the CPC, the pleading rules and the High Court rules.
- [ ] No non-joinder or misjoinder of parties or causes of action.
- [ ] Any minor properly represented (next friend / guardian).
- [ ] Signed and verified.
- [ ] Within the court's jurisdiction (pecuniary, territorial, subject-matter).
- [ ] Not liable to rejection (see O.VII R.11 below).
- [ ] Documents listed in the prescribed form and in order.
- [ ] Address for service of the plaintiff.
- [ ] Names and addresses of legal representatives where relevant.
- [ ] Limitation: within time, or the plaint shows the ground of exemption.
- [ ] Relief clause proper, specific and complete.
- [ ] Correct court fee affixed.

**Written statement**
- [ ] Filed at or before the first hearing; ordinarily within 30 days.
- [ ] All documents in the defendant's possession or control that the defence relies on, attached.
- [ ] List of relied-upon documents not in the defendant's possession.

## 2. Written statement

| Rule | Text (CPC) | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| O.VIII R.1 | WS "at or before the first hearing or within such time as the Court may permit"; proviso: period "shall not ordinarily exceed thirty days" | Diary the 30 days from service; seek time by application with reasons. |
| O.VIII R.3 | "It shall not be sufficient for a defendant in his written statement to deny generally… the defendant must deal specifically with each allegation of fact of which he does not admit the truth, except damages." | Parawise reply, one numbered reply per plaint paragraph. |
| O.VIII R.4 | Denial must not be evasive; "answer the point of substance". | Deny the substance (e.g. "received that sum or any part thereof"), not just the wording. |
| O.VIII R.5 | Allegation not denied specifically or by necessary implication, or stated "not admitted", "shall be taken to be admitted". | Use "denied" or "not admitted" for every paragraph not admitted. |
| O.VIII R.6 | Set-off: in a suit for recovery of money, an ascertained sum; WS then has "the same effect as a plaint in a cross-suit". | Only for money claims. |

**Counter-claim: caution.** The federal CPC text has **no counter-claim rule** (no O.VIII R.6-A; only set-off
in R.6). A counter-claim in a written statement is entertained by courts on case law, treated as a cross-suit,
so it must carry everything a plaint needs: cause of action, valuation, court fee, prayer and verification
[case law: confirm authority before relying on it]. If in doubt, file a separate suit and apply for joint
hearing.

Also include: preliminary objections (maintainability, limitation, jurisdiction, non-joinder, valuation and
court fee, cause of action, O.VII R.11 grounds), additional pleas, list of documents, verification.

## 3. Rejection of plaint: O.VII R.11

Text: "The plaint shall be rejected in the following cases:
(a) where it does not disclose a cause of action;
(b) where the relief claimed is under valued, and the plaintiff, on being required by the Court to correct
the valuation within a time to be fixed by the Court, fails to do so;
(c) where the relief claimed is properly valued, but the plaint is written upon paper insufficiently stamped,
and the plaintiff, on being required by the Court to supply the requisite stamp-paper within a time to be
fixed by the Court, fails to do so;
(d) where the suit appears from the statement in the plaint to be barred by any law."

- R.12: the judge records an order with reasons. R.13: rejection does **not** by itself bar a fresh plaint
  on the same cause of action.
- The court looks at **the plaint and its documents**, read as true; the defence is not considered
  [case law: confirm authority].
- Common "barred by law" heads (cite the exact provision): limitation (Limitation Act s.3 and the Article),
  res judicata (CPC s.11), bar after withdrawal without permission (O.XXIII R.1(3)), bar of civil court
  jurisdiction in revenue matters (Land Revenue Act s.172), 12(2) bar on a separate suit (CPC s.12(2)).
- (b) and (c) need a prior court order to correct valuation or stamp; ask for that order first.

## 4. Application under Section 12(2) CPC

Text: "Where a person challenges the validity of a judgment, decree or order on the plea of fraud,
mis-representation or want of jurisdiction, he shall seek his remedy by making an application to the Court
which passed the final judgment, decree or order and not by a separate suit."

- [ ] File in **the court that passed the final order** (not a fresh suit).
- [ ] Plead **fraud / misrepresentation / want of jurisdiction** with particulars: who, what, when, how.
- [ ] Limitation: Limitation Act **Article 181**, "Applications for which no period of limitation is provided
      elsewhere", **three years** from "when the right to apply accrues"; where fraud is concealed, time from
      knowledge [case law: confirm authority].
- [ ] Supporting affidavit; annex the impugned order and the documents proving the fraud.
- [ ] If facts are disputed, the court may frame issues and record evidence [case law: confirm].
- [ ] Pair with a s.151 application to call for the court's own record where needed.

## 5. Impleading parties: O.I R.10

- R.10(2): the Court "may at any stage of the proceedings, either upon or without the application of either
  party… order that the name of any party improperly joined… be struck out, and that the name of any person
  who ought to have been joined… or whose presence before the Court may be necessary in order to enable the
  Court effectually and completely to adjudicate upon and settle all the questions involved in the suit, be
  added."
- R.10(4): where a defendant is added, the plaint is amended and served on the new defendant.
- R.10(5): as against the added defendant, proceedings begin only on service of summons (Limitation Act s.22).
- Plead: why the person is a **necessary** party (relief against him) or a **proper** party (presence
  needed to decide all questions); attach the document showing his interest (e.g. a sale deed).

## 6. Amendment of pleadings: O.VI R.17

- Text: the Court "may at any stage of the proceedings allow either party to alter or amend his pleadings…
  and all such amendments shall be made as may be necessary for the purpose of determining the real
  questions in controversy between the parties."
- R.18: if leave is granted and no time fixed, amend within **14 days**, or the right lapses unless extended.
- Attach the proposed amended paragraphs; show no change in the nature of the suit and no time-barred claim
  is introduced [case law: confirm].

## 7. Temporary injunction: O.XXXIX R.1 and R.2

- R.1: where it is proved by affidavit or otherwise that property in dispute is in danger of being "wasted,
  damaged or alienated by any party to the suit, or wrongfully sold in execution of a decree", or the
  defendant threatens to dispose of his property to defraud creditors.
- R.2: in a suit to restrain a breach of contract or other injury, the plaintiff may apply "at any time after
  the commencement of the suit" to restrain the breach or injury.
- The three ingredients courts apply, **prima facie case, balance of convenience, irreparable loss**, come
  from case law, not the rule text [case law: confirm authority]. Plead each under its own heading.
- Supporting affidavit; documents showing title or possession and the threat.

## 8. Withdrawal and its bar: O.XXIII R.1

- R.1(1): plaintiff may withdraw or abandon part of the claim at any time.
- R.1(2): permission to withdraw **with liberty to file afresh** only for a formal defect or other
  sufficient grounds.
- **R.1(3)**: withdrawal **without** that permission makes the plaintiff liable for costs and "precluded from
  instituting any fresh suit in respect of such subject-matter".
- R.1(4): one of several plaintiffs cannot be allowed to withdraw without the others' consent.

## 9. Counsel on record: O.III

- R.1: any appearance, application or act may be made "by the party in person, or by his recognized agent, or
  by a pleader"; the Court may direct appearance in person.
- **R.4(2)**: every appointment of a pleader "shall be deemed to be in force until determined with the leave
  of the Court by a writing signed by the client or the pleader, as the case may be, and filed in Court, or
  until the client or the pleader dies, or until all proceedings in the suit are ended so far as regards the
  client."

## 10. Stay of later suit: Section 10

"No Court shall proceed with the trial of any suit in which the matter in issue is also directly and
substantially in issue in a previously instituted suit between the same parties, or between parties under
whom they or any of them claim litigating under the same title…". Test: same matter directly and
substantially in issue, same parties, earlier suit pending. If the later suit raises wider issues, ask for a
joint hearing instead.

## 11. Inherent power: Section 151

Includes the 2020 federal amendment: inherent power "to be exercised after recording reasons" (Act VII of
2020). Use only where no specific provision applies.

## 12. Affidavits and identification (Sindh practice)

- Affidavits in Hyderabad civil courts are sworn at the **Identity Section (ISMS)**: biometric verification,
  photograph, printed affidavit signed before the court staff and the Commissioner for Taking Affidavits.
- The ISMS form has columns for the **identifying advocate** (signature, name, ledger number): have counsel
  identify the deponent.
- Verification of pleadings and affidavits: state what is on personal knowledge and what is on information
  or belief.
