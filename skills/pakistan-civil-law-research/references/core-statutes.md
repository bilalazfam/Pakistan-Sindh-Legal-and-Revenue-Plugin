# Core Pakistani Civil Statutes — Question-to-Act Map

Use this to pin a legal question to the controlling Act(s) and the specific provisions, then state the
statutory ingredients as a checklist for the application step. Section numbers are the commonly cited ones;
always confirm the current text, because provincial amendments (especially in Sindh) can alter or renumber
them. Where a matter is provincial, the **Sindh** instrument governs Sindh cases.

## 1. Contract & specific performance
- **Contract Act 1872** — formation, consideration, free consent, void/voidable agreements, breach and
  damages (ss. 10, 23–25 validity; ss. 73–74 damages).
- **Specific Relief Act 1877** — s. 12 (when specific performance is granted), s. 22 (discretion of the
  court — the plaintiff must show readiness and willingness), ss. 39–42 (cancellation of instruments,
  declaratory decrees), s. 42 (declaration of status/right), ss. 54–56 (injunctions).
- *Typical ingredients for specific performance*: a concluded contract, certainty of terms, consideration,
  plaintiff's continuous readiness and willingness, and no bar under s. 22 discretion.

## 2. Property, transfer & gift (hiba)
- **Transfer of Property Act 1882** — sale (s. 54), the distinction between an agreement to sell and a sale,
  conditions, mortgage, lease, and the doctrine of *lis pendens* (s. 52).
- **Muhammadan Law (hiba)** — a valid gift requires the three essentials: (i) declaration by the donor,
  (ii) acceptance by the donee, and (iii) delivery of possession. Oral gifts are recognised, but their
  *proof* is scrutinised strictly, especially where a written/registered instrument is later set up.
- Cross-check gift questions against **Registration Act 1908** (below) and **Qanun-e-Shahadat** on proof.

## 3. Registration & documents
- **Registration Act 1908** — s. 17 (instruments for which registration is compulsory, including instruments
  affecting immovable property of value ≥ Rs. 100), s. 49 (effect of non-registration — an unregistered
  document that must be registered cannot affect immovable property or be received in evidence of the
  transaction), s. 47 (time from which a registered document operates).
- Use this whenever the authenticity, timing, or admissibility of a deed, gift entry, or mutation is in issue.

## 4. Inheritance & succession
- **Muhammadan Law** governs succession for Muslims — shares of heirs, the rule against a gift/bequest
  defeating heirs, and the treatment of oral dispositions.
- **Succession Act 1925** — letters of administration, probate, and succession certificates.
- Relevant where a *hiba* or mutation is alleged to have diverted property away from legal heirs (e.g. a
  challenged gift entry in an estate).

## 5. Pre-emption
- Pre-emption (*shufa*) is provincial. Identify the applicable provincial pre-emption law and its
  ingredients — the classes of pre-emptor, the requirement and proof of the *talbs* (demands), and the
  limitation period, which is short and strictly enforced.
- Confirm which pre-emption statute currently governs in Sindh before stating ingredients.

## 6. Partnership & rendition of accounts
- **Partnership Act 1932** — s. 4 (definition), ss. 9–13 (mutual rights and duties, duty of good faith and
  to render true accounts), s. 37 (share/interest on dissolution), and dissolution (ss. 39–44).
- A rendition-of-accounts suit typically proceeds in two stages: a preliminary decree establishing the right
  to accounts, then accounting and a final decree for the sum found due.

## 7. Land revenue & tenancy (Sindh)
- **Sindh Land Revenue Act 1967** — record of rights, mutation/entry procedure, and the presumption of truth
  attaching to revenue entries (and how it is rebutted).
- **Colonization of Government Lands (Sindh) Act 1912** and grant conditions — relevant to barrage/colony
  land holdings.
- **Land reform** instruments where ancestral agricultural land is in issue: MLR 115/1972, Land Reforms Act
  1977, and the constitutional jurisprudence (e.g. *Qazalbash Waqf*).

## 8. Limitation
- **Limitation Act 1908** — the Schedule articles fix the period for each kind of suit/appeal; s. 3 makes
  limitation a mandatory bar the court applies even if not pleaded; ss. 5 (condonation), 14 (exclusion of
  time in bona fide proceedings), and 18 (fraud) are the common escapes. Always check limitation early — it
  can dispose of a matter regardless of merits.

## 9. Evidence (Qanun-e-Shahadat Order 1984)
- Burden and standard of proof, admissibility of documents, the requirement to produce the maker/attesting
  witnesses (Art. 79 for documents required by law to be attested), presumptions, and the treatment of
  secondary evidence. Central to attacking a fabricated gift entry or an unproved deed.

## 10. Civil procedure (CPC 1908) — the Orders you actually use
- **Order VI** — pleadings generally; **Order VI Rule 17** amendment of pleadings.
- **Order VII** — plaint; Rule 11 rejection of plaint.
- **Order VIII** — written statement, set-off, counter-claim; parawise reply.
- **Order I / Order II** — parties, joinder, and frame of suit.
- **Order XXXVII** — summary suits on negotiable instruments/liquidated demands.
- **Order XXXVIII** — arrest and **attachment before judgment**.
- **Order XXXIX Rules 1–2** — temporary injunctions (the three tests: prima facie case, balance of
  convenience, irreparable loss).
- **Order XL** — appointment of a receiver.
- **Section 12(2)** — setting aside a decree/order obtained by fraud or misrepresentation.
- **Section 151** — inherent powers.

## 11. Fees & stamps
- **Court Fees Act 1870** and **Stamp Act 1899** (as applied in Sindh) — correct valuation and duty; a
  wrongly valued or under-stamped instrument can be challenged and affects admissibility.

## 12. Special civil remedies
- **Illegal Dispossession Act 2005** — summary remedy against unlawful dispossession of immovable property by
  land-grabbers (note its criminal-civil hybrid character).
- **Financial Institutions (Recovery of Finances) Ordinance 2001** — bank recovery suits (specialised forum).
- **Constitution Article 199** — constitutional petition before the High Court where no other adequate remedy
  exists (used for revenue/land-commission and regulatory matters).
